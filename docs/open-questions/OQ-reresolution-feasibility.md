# OQ-reresolution-feasibility: When is reopening a past resolution feasible?

- **ID:** OQ-reresolution-feasibility
- **Status:** open

## Question

When a policy or legal-corpus change would alter the conclusion of a solved, closed, redirected or stuck problem, which feasibility criteria decide whether it actually reopens (T23)?

## Why it matters

Re-resolution improves the rules for every past case (D-59), but reopening problems that people finished can confuse, burden or undo lawful work. Too strict leaves wrong conclusions in place; too loose reopens everything on every pack change.

## Current default (what we built meanwhile)

Reopen only when the conclusion changes and all hold: the problem still exists, the jurisdiction is still enabled, the original initiator or a steward can be notified, and reopening does not undo a lawful completed implementation without a new proposal. Otherwise write a re-resolution record and change no state. Never silent, history kept, appealable (`RERESOLVE-1`). Review queue limits and batching are pack values.

## Who can help

Governance researchers; case-management and legal-process practitioners; trust and safety practitioners.

## What a good answer looks like

A criteria list with worked examples (a reopened solved problem, an infeasible one, a lawful completed implementation) and a rule for batching large replays.
