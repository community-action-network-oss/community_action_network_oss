# OQ-supranational-default: Is the binding supranational layer (L2) the right default, and for which jurisdictions?

- **ID:** OQ-supranational-default
- **Status:** open

## Question

Please confirm that L2 is applied wherever a supranational body binds the jurisdiction (for the Netherlands: the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, EU law and the ECHR), sits between UN human rights and the national constitution, and is skipped where nothing binds.

## Why it matters

The founder named the layers but the supranational placement and the Dutch instruments are a default, not a stated decision (D-61). Getting it wrong changes what is lawful for every Amsterdam problem.

## Current default (what we built meanwhile)

L2 applies where binding, with the EU Charter, EU law and the ECHR for the Netherlands, and is skipped for jurisdictions with no binding body. Each L2 corpus carries provenance (`LEGAL-SOURCE-1`).

## Who can help

EU and Council of Europe law specialists; Dutch constitutional lawyers; comparative-law researchers.

## What a good answer looks like

A confirmation or correction of the layer order for the Netherlands, and a list of other supranational bodies to model later.
