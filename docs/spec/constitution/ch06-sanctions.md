# Chapter VI. Restrictions, sanctions, and restoration

Binds slice 1 minimally. Rewritten from Arts 41-43 and 50.

### VI.1 PROPORTIONATE-RESTRICT: Proportionate restrictions
*Status: Drafted (Art 41); Decided (Art 42) · Old: Art 41, 42 · First phase: S1-min, P5*

Restrictions rest on behavior and risk, never on disagreement, unpopular identity, political position, religion, geography, disability, language style, education, or lawful criticism of the platform. From least to most restrictive:

1. Contextual reminder or educational prompt
2. Required revision before publication
3. Additional evidence or reasoning requirement
4. Slower posting rate or cooldown
5. Additional review
6. Limited visibility pending review
7. Temporary loss of participation in a topic or role
8. Removal from expert, reviewer, moderator, or steward eligibility
9. Temporary platform suspension

Nothing on this ladder is permanent without a restoration path (VI.3). Permanent loss of an access class needs strong evidence, independent review, and a higher threshold, and it stays open to review and appeal. Restrictions are necessary, proportionate, as narrow as practical, time-bound where possible, and accompanied by reasons and an appeal route. Severe imminent threats, exploitation, malware, coordinated manipulation, or credible safety risks may justify immediate temporary restriction while review proceeds.

A flag is a risk signal and not proof of wrongdoing. Flags state the behavior, source, confidence, scope, and expiry. Protected characteristics and lawful viewpoints are not risk features. A successful appeal removes or corrects the associated signals. Reviewers do not see irrelevant reputation before judging. Extra review must evaluate content fairly rather than hunt for reasons to reject. Restrictions decay or are reviewed periodically. Audits test false positives and unequal impact. Reading public information stays available unless safety, legal duty, evasion prevention, or protection of targets requires narrower access. The system separates inability, mistake, uncertainty, disagreement, negligence, recklessness, deliberate abuse, coordinated manipulation, and imminent threat, and these do not get the same consequence.

Rules: RESTORE-1, APPEAL-2

### VI.2 SANCTION-ABUSE: Sanctions target demonstrable abuse
*Status: Decided · Old: Art 43 · First phase: S1*

Penalties are for users who game the system, commit or facilitate illegal acts, manipulate reviews, evade safeguards, falsify evidence, coordinate abuse, or otherwise act maliciously. Differences of opinion, unpopular positions, uncertainty, honest mistakes, and good-faith criticism are not sanctionable. Decisions name the conduct and do not infer bad intent from disagreement. When intent is unclear, respond to observable risk with the least restrictive effective measure.

The deliberate participation standard (II.5) defines behavior-specific tolerance buffers: posting frequency and repetition; similarity and coordination in campaigns; knowingly false claims, fabricated sources, or impersonation; attempts to probe or bypass moderation; undisclosed conflicts in consequential reviews; harassment, threats, doxxing, exploitation, malware, or illegal facilitation; collusive labeling or Sybil behavior; reposting of materially unchanged rejected content. Crossing a threshold creates a reviewable risk flag and not a conclusion about character. Each flag states the trigger, threshold, evidence, confidence, scope, expiry, and next action. Clear technical abuse may be blocked at once, while ambiguous signals need corroboration before severe restriction. Buffers are versioned, measurable, context-sensitive, and probe-resistant. Exact thresholds may stay private when disclosure would ease evasion, while the prohibited behavior, principle, range of enforcement, and appeal rights stay public.

Rules: APPEAL-2

### VI.3 DEACTIVATE-RESTORE: Deactivation and restoration
*Status: Decided · Old: Art 50 · First phase: P5*

The network deactivates rather than expels irreversibly. A person, role, credential, node, or service may be deactivated, isolated, and required to remediate, and appeal and restoration stay possible. Restoration may require explanation of the triggering evidence, independent review, remediation of the weakness, fresh keys or credentials, a clean state, revalidation of affected records, temporarily reduced privileges, and monitoring. The right to appeal does not require immediate reconnection while a credible security or safety risk remains active. Full account exclusion is therefore always deactivation with a restoration route, never a dead end. Phase 5.

Rules: RESTORE-1
