# Chapter I. Purpose and scope

Binds slice 1. Rewritten from Arts 1-3, 19, 25, 29, 51-52, 56-58, 64 (second paragraph), 71 and 98. Mechanics live in the spec sections; this chapter holds the principle and the rule IDs that enforce it.

### I.1 PURPOSE-RESOLUTION: Purpose and direction
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 1, 19, 52, 56 · First phase: S1*

The platform exists to help the public identify, understand, and resolve civic and structural problems through transparent participatory problem-solving, collective intelligence, lawful coordination, implementation tracking, and verified learning.

- The unit of work is the public problem, never the individual client.
- The platform optimizes for verified public resolution. It does not optimize for personalized service, content consumption, attention, posting volume, or discussion volume.
- Every feature, metric, model, policy, and partnership must show how it advances verified resolution while preserving rights, safety, privacy, contestability, and accountability. A feature that raises engagement without improving resolution is not built for that reason.
- The platform coordinates resolution capacity and does not claim to hold every solution. It helps communities classify an issue, organize evidence, identify affected groups, map responsible roles and institutions, compare lawful interventions, form stewardship, coordinate implementation, expose blockers, and verify outcomes. It claims no jurisdiction, professional authority, investigative power, or operational competence it does not have.
- Capacity comes from people with lived experience, local participants, qualified experts, prior resolution records, institutions, public agencies and lawful processes, crisis resources, implementation volunteers, and outcome verifiers. Human beings remain the source of responsibility, consent, implementation, and accountable judgment (see V.1).
- The platform stays improvable by design. New capabilities may extend implementation. They must not silently redefine this purpose.

### I.2 RIGHTS-PRECEDENCE: Rights core and precedence order
*Status: Decided · Old: Art 25 · First phase: S1*

**Protected core.** No ordinary amendment, policy pack, jurisdiction pack, or operator setting may remove these:

- Human dignity and equal moral worth
- Nonviolence
- Equal protection and non-discrimination
- Protection of children and vulnerable people
- Protection against concealed, externalized, or unjustly transferred harm
- Privacy, data minimization, and informed consent
- Freedom of thought, belief, expression, peaceful assembly, and association, subject to lawful and rights-respecting limits
- Meaningful participation in matters affecting a person or community
- Accessibility and inclusion
- Explanation, contestability, remedy, and appeal
- No forced participation
- No collective punishment
- No sale of influence over problem visibility, moderation, or solution outcomes
- No engagement maximization as the supreme objective
- No autonomous AI sovereignty
- Transparency of consequential rules, evidence standards, and conflicts of interest
- The right to leave and to request data deletion where legally possible
- Protection of affected parties, minorities, nonparticipants, future generations, and ecosystems

**Reference stack.** Enforceable rules should map to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the ICCPR, the ICESCR, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination, the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, and the OHCHR B-Tech Project. These instruments differ in legal status and ratification. Treat them as a principled minimum, then map binding obligations and reservations per jurisdiction. Qualified human-rights and legal review is required before source text becomes an enforceable rule.

**Precedence order (new).** When two rules conflict, the higher tier wins:

1. Rights (the protected core above)
2. Crisis and safety
3. Privacy
4. Legal gate (IV.5)
5. Procedure (workflow, lifecycle, deadlines)
6. Ranking and prominence

A lower tier never overrides a higher one. Within a tier, the stricter protection applies. If the conflict cannot be settled mechanically, the item fails closed (V.4) and goes to a human, and the decision record cites both rule IDs.

**Change protection.** The protected core cannot be removed or weakened by the ordinary amendment procedure (VIII.2). No extraordinary procedure exists yet, so until one is ratified the core is frozen.

Rules: PREC-1

### I.3 SCOPE-PUBLIC-ONLY: Public problems only
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 57, 71 · First phase: S1*

The platform does not operate private professional workflows. It provides no therapy, legal representation, medical treatment, private consulting, missing-person investigation, or any one-to-one service. It assigns no therapist, doctor, lawyer, consultant, investigator, or other provider to an individual.

- A transient intake may point to an external category of help or official resource, then delete the personal draft.
- Professionals take part only as public contributors, reviewers, experts, implementers, or institutional representatives inside a public problem.
- Commercial providers may contribute under conflict-of-interest and anti-solicitation rules. The platform is never a lead-generation marketplace, and recommendations cannot be bought or ranked by payment.
- Wording elsewhere in the spec that implies individual help ("deserves help", "seek help", "problem owner's context") is read subject to this article.

Rules: SCOPE-1

### I.4 SIGNAL-TO-PUBLIC: Individual signal becomes a public problem
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 2, 58 · First phase: S1*

One person's experience may reveal a genuine public problem. It can open a systemic hypothesis, support an evidence tier, attract investigation, or corroborate a recurring pattern.

- A personal experience supports a public problem only after it becomes a non-identifying structural statement. The record names the affected class or geography, the institutional or systemic mechanism, the public significance, the evidence tier, and the missing evidence. It keeps no personal narrative, identity, private chronology, or one-to-one request.
- One report justifies a hypothesis. It does not establish prevalence or causation.
- Minority and first-reported problems keep their significance. Every good-faith public problem gets procedural dignity: evidence strength affects confidence, severity and rights affect importance, and popularity affects neither truth nor moral worth.
- Submitting requires an account (II.2). "Anyone may submit" means anyone may hold an account, not that anonymous writes are accepted. Slice 1 writes are invite-only.

Rules: SCOPE-1, RANK-1

### I.5 TAXONOMY-CLOSED: Taxonomy (closed list)
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 3 · First phase: S1*

The platform classifies public problems into a closed list. Adding a category is a policy-pack change (VIII.2), not an ad hoc act.

- Neighborhood or local civic problem
- Municipal or regional problem
- State, national, or international systemic problem
- Institutional or organizational failure
- Public infrastructure or service problem
- Public health, environmental, economic, educational, or safety pattern
- Legal, regulatory, policy, or constitutional reform problem
- Preparation and resilience problem
- Platform governance problem (not in slice 1)

`investigation_needed` is not a category. It is a flag derived from the evidence tier (III.4).

Personal, medical, legal, therapeutic, commercial, and emergency requests are out of scope as collaborative problems. They get a transient external route (I.6). Their non-identifying structural signal may be submitted separately as a public problem (I.4). An out-of-scope submission is declined with a reason code from a closed ineligibility list held in the policy pack.

Rules: SCOPE-1

### I.6 CRISIS-ROUTING: Crisis routing that always works
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 51 · First phase: S1*

A personal crisis is never published as a collaborative problem. If intake detects suicidal thoughts, imminent harm, abuse, a medical emergency, or another individualized safety need, the platform stops public submission and shows jurisdiction-appropriate external resources. It does not diagnose, treat, match a private professional, crowdsource the crisis, locate the person, or claim it can intervene.

- The transient disclosure may become a separate non-identifying systemic hypothesis only when the result has no personal narrative and no link back to the person.
- Static safety routing is served independent of moderation and of every other dependency. If moderation fails, the crisis page still loads.

Rules: CRISIS-STATIC-1, SCOPE-1

### I.7 NONMONETARY-INDEPENDENT: Independence and non-monetary operation
*Status: Drafted (Art 29); Decided (Art 98) · Old: Art 29, 98 · First phase: S1*

The platform itself must not collect, hold, transfer, distribute, account for, intermediate, or optimize money. It has no subscriptions, fees, paywalls, donations, fundraising, dues, advertising, sponsorship auctions, wallets, balances, tokens, escrow, bounties, cash rewards, paid placement, revenue sharing, or treasury. Ordinary people use the civic workflow without payment.

Independence rules:

- No payment influences which solution is chosen or shown as valid.
- No sale of personal or behavioral data.
- No advertising or paid placement in rankings, and no paid suppression or promotion of problems.
- No undisclosed commercial influence over expert recommendations.
- No government, political-party, host, or corporate control over moderation outcomes.
- No access to private data without consent or valid legal process.
- No retaliation against lawful criticism of the platform or its operators.
- Material financial, professional, institutional, and political conflicts of interest are disclosed and feed into participation, review assignment, weighting, and governance.

In-kind support (hosting, labor, translation, expertise) earns factual acknowledgment only, never financial credit, ownership, governance weight, data access, moderation authority, ranking influence, or protocol privilege. Recognition is factual, proportionate, non-tracking, non-transferable, conflict-disclosed, and separate from civic content.

Real-world solutions may need funding or procurement. Those processes stay outside the platform. The platform may document the need, the authority, the external route, procedural status, and implementation evidence. It never becomes a payment, crowdfunding, contracting, or financial-accountability system. Support concentration is disclosed, and the project must survive the withdrawal or misconduct of any major host. Node-level mechanics are in IX.4 and spec §20A.

Rules: MONEY-0
