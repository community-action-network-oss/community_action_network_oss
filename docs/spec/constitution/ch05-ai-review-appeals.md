# Chapter V. AI, review, and appeals

Binds slice 1. Rewritten from Arts 15, 20, 23, 26, 31-32, 44-45, 64, 97.

### V.1 AI-INSTRUMENT: AI is an instrument
*Status: Position (Art 15); Decided (Art 26) · Old: Art 15, 26 · First phase: S1*

AI may classify, retrieve, compare, question, translate, summarize, flag, explain, and recommend. It must not manufacture authority or become the final source of truth, law, expertise, consent, or political legitimacy. Consequential AI output exposes evidence, uncertainty, assumptions, model and policy versions, and review paths, and users can contest it. Higher potential harm needs stronger evidence and more meaningful human oversight.

Solutions are driven by people, not generated by AI. AI reduces friction: it structures information, retrieves earlier records, surfaces contradictions, translates, and tests claims. It does not replace lived experience, judgment, responsibility, or implementation.

**Slice 1 runs no live AI.** It uses deterministic checks plus human review of everything published, and that includes contributions as well as problems. The AI gateway interface exists behind a flag that stays off. Turning it on needs a data-protection impact assessment and founder approval (spec §14). The manual path is the only path today.

**Review is not an entry exam.** Contributors are not required to prove understanding before taking part. Any participation check must be task- and risk-relevant, available in the contributor's language, accessible, explainable and appealable, able to recognize lived and local knowledge, separate from credential verification, designed to teach rather than exclude, and tested for disparate impact (see VII.2).

Rules: AI-OFF-1

### V.2 BOUNDED-INFERENCE: Bounded inference
*Status: Decided · Old: Art 97 · First phase: S1-min*

When AI is on, it never receives the whole constitution, every jurisdiction's law, all workflow rules, and the full history in each request. It receives the smallest applicable rule set, typed bounded stages, and escalation when splitting would drop a mandatory dependency or exception.

- Stable public policy is cached in canonical form. Personal or restricted content needs isolated namespaces, minimal retention, encryption, authorization re-checks, and deletion propagation. A cache hit never bypasses current law, policy, consent, authorization, or publication review.
- Route to deterministic code or a valid cache first, then the cheapest approved model that passed required task, language, privacy, context, and risk thresholds. Escalate to stronger models and qualified humans as confidence, coverage, safety, or consequence demand. Cost never buys weaker privacy or protection.
- Model choice follows versioned evaluations and production outcomes, not brand or benchmark publicity. Measure policy coverage, exception handling, PII safety, grounding, calibration, schema reliability, multilingual performance, human correction, latency, cache behavior, and cost per safe accepted result.
- No model authorizes its own consequential action, counts as an independent human approval, or silently routes data to an unapproved provider, region, retention policy, or version. Mechanics are in spec §14.

Rules: AI-OFF-1

### V.3 MACHINE-RULES: Machine-enforceable layer
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 20 · First phase: S1*

The human-readable constitution is accompanied by a versioned policy representation with: stable article and rule IDs; scope and jurisdiction; required and prohibited actions; decision authority; evidence requirements; exceptions and emergency rules; escalation and appeal paths; priority and conflict rules (I.2); effective dates and amendment history; and positive, negative, boundary, multilingual, and adversarial tests. Automated workflows retrieve and apply the relevant rules, produce structured decision records, and fail safely when rules conflict or context is missing. The seed of that layer is `rules.md`.

Rules: PREC-1

### V.4 PREPUB-FAILCLOSED: Pre-publication review, fail closed, and the interim moderator
*Status: Decided (Art 31, 45); Drafted (Art 64) · Old: Art 31, 45, 64 · First phase: S1*

Nothing enters public view until it has been reviewed. Slice 1 uses the privacy gate (II.6) plus a human moderator. Phase 5 adds panels and, if enabled, AI first-pass review.

- **Fail closed.** If moderation is unavailable, outdated, unsupported in the language, or below confidence, the item stays a private draft. It can be re-routed to another reviewer or pool, and the reviewer queue may be slow rather than lower the standard. Public exposure fails closed. Safety routing fails open (I.6).
- **Every moderation decision** carries `rule_ids[]`, a field or span reference, a `revision_hint` (null only if safety-sensitive), the policy version, and `appealable_until`. The UI shows each hint next to its field and offers revise-and-resubmit, which keeps the draft.
- **Visible states** distinguish: withdrawn by user; revision requested; awaiting review; awaiting specific expertise; awaiting jurisdictional review; approved on appeal; rejected with reasons; closed for demonstrated abuse. Withdrawal and resubmission is allowed. Reposting to probe or evade safeguards is not, and revisions are compared using the salted fingerprint (II.6).
- **Appeals.** A user may appeal any rejection or revise decision. The reviewer must differ from the submitter and from the original decider when the moderator pool has two or more. With one moderator the appeal is allowed and the "same interim moderator" is disclosed to the appellant. An unsuccessful good-faith appeal is never penalized. A successful appeal corrects the content decision and produces a candidate example for V.5.
- **Interim-moderator clause.** Until moderator panels exist, a single moderator may decide. Each such decision is audited, is shown as "Interim decision, will be re-reviewed", and is queued for re-review once panels exist. Interim status is removed only by a re-review decision.

Rules: PUB-FAILCLOSED-1, MOD-EXPLAIN-1, INTERIM-1, APPEAL-1, APPEAL-2, DRAFT-TTL-1, CRISIS-STATIC-1

### V.5 APPEALS-FEEDBACK: Appeals feed the next rules-review cycle
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 44 · First phase: P5*

A successful appeal does not rewrite grounding data, prompts, models, or rules. It creates a versioned candidate example for the next review cycle. Before use, a candidate needs privacy review and redaction, independent extra labeling, coordination and poisoning checks, failure analysis, regression tests, disparate-impact evaluation, formal approval, and versioned rollout with rollback. An unsuccessful good-faith appeal carries no penalty, and penalties need separate evidence of deliberate abuse. Appeal outcomes write nothing to grounding tables directly. Phase 5.

Rules: APPEAL-1

### V.6 DISTRIBUTED-REVIEW: Distributed review and consensus
*Status: Decided (Art 23); Drafted (Art 32) · Old: Art 23, 32 · First phase: P5*

No single AI output, reviewer, popularity signal, or reputation score becomes final authority. Contested or consequential items go to distributed human review: randomized and context-masked assignment; domain and jurisdiction matching where needed; conflict declaration and detection; independent labels before reviewers see the aggregate; multiple reviewers for consequential decisions; disagreement measurement and escalation; fresh pools on appeal; calibration on known cases; provenance without exposing reviewer data. Reputation may shift confidence and is never proof that a person is right.

Consensus is more than a count. It weighs independence, competence, calibration, conflicts, evidence quality, contextual fit, and uncertainty. Disagreement is recorded, not forced into unanimity. For high-impact items thresholds are set before labels are collected. Ties, low confidence, coordinated anomalies, and unresolved rights conflicts escalate. Slice 1 uses a single moderator pool instead. Phase 5.
