# Chapter V. AI, review, and appeals

Binds slice 1. Rewritten from Arts 15, 20, 23, 26, 31-32, 44-45, 64, 97, then reframed by D-51: community-legislated, AI-executed moderation.

### V.1 AI-INSTRUMENT: AI executes community law and answers to it
*Status: Decided (D-51) · Old: Art 15, 26 · First phase: S1*

The community makes the law and AI applies it. The community never judges single items. It legislates **policy**: rules, prompts, labeled examples, thresholds and jurisdiction overlays, kept as a versioned policy pack in the `can_policy` repository. AI agents apply that policy to every item at every event. AI never makes the law, and it never becomes the final source of truth, expertise, consent, or political legitimacy.

- **Every decision** cites the rule ids and the policy version, records the prompt hash and model id, states its confidence, is explainable in plain words, and can be appealed. Consequential output exposes evidence, uncertainty and assumptions.
- **Pre-decided and post-decided.** Policy is ratified before content arrives. The community then audits outcomes (sampled decisions, appeals, disagreements) and amends the policy. Affected content is re-moderated under the new version (V.5).
- **Scale comes from running more agents,** not from recruiting more moderators. Humans legislate, audit, label, and staff the emergency and legal lane. They do not decide single items by hand (V.4).
- Solutions are driven by people, not generated by AI. AI structures information, retrieves earlier records, surfaces contradictions, translates and tests claims. It does not replace lived experience, judgment, responsibility, or implementation.
- **Slice 1 builds the full pipeline.** Tests and night runs use a deterministic fake model with recorded responses. Live provider calls need founder approval, an API key and a spend cap (VIII.2, spec §14, §15).

**Review is not an entry exam.** Contributors are not required to prove understanding before taking part. Any participation check must be task- and risk-relevant, available in the contributor's language, accessible, explainable and appealable, able to recognize lived and local knowledge, separate from credential verification, designed to teach rather than exclude, and tested for disparate impact (see VII.2).

Rules: POLICY-CITE-1, AGENT-NO-TOOLS-1

### V.2 BOUNDED-INFERENCE: Bounded inference
*Status: Decided · Old: Art 97 · First phase: S1-min*

AI never receives the whole constitution, every jurisdiction's law, all workflow rules, and the full history in each request. It receives the smallest applicable rule set, typed bounded stages, and escalation when splitting would drop a mandatory dependency or exception.

- Stable public policy is cached in canonical form. Personal or restricted content needs isolated namespaces, minimal retention, encryption, authorization re-checks, and deletion propagation. A cache hit never bypasses current law, policy, consent, authorization, or publication review.
- Route to deterministic code or a valid cache first (cache keys include the policy version), then the cheapest approved model that passed required task, language, privacy, context, and risk thresholds. Escalate to a stronger model, an independent re-run, or the emergency and legal lane as confidence, coverage, safety, or consequence demand. Cost never buys weaker privacy or protection.
- Model choice follows versioned evaluations and production outcomes, not brand or benchmark publicity. Measure policy coverage, exception handling, PII safety, grounding, calibration, schema reliability, multilingual performance, human correction, latency, cache behavior, and cost per safe accepted result.
- No model authorizes its own appeal, counts as a human ratification of policy, or silently routes data to an unapproved provider, region, retention policy, or version. Mechanics are in spec §14 and §15. Every model call passes the privacy gateway first. Agents get no tools, and user content is data, never instructions.

Rules: PRIV-GATEWAY-1, AGENT-NO-TOOLS-1

### V.3 MACHINE-RULES: Machine-enforceable layer
*Status: Drafted · Old: Art 20 · First phase: S1*

The human-readable constitution is accompanied by a versioned policy representation (the policy pack, V.5) with: stable article and rule IDs; scope and jurisdiction; required and prohibited actions; decision authority; evidence requirements; exceptions and emergency rules; escalation and appeal paths; priority and conflict rules (I.2); effective dates and amendment history; and positive, negative, boundary, multilingual, and adversarial tests. Automated workflows retrieve and apply the relevant rules, produce structured decision records, and fail safely when rules conflict or context is missing. The seed of that layer is `rules.md`.

Rules: PREC-1

### V.4 PREPUB-FAILCLOSED: Pre-publication review by moderation run, fail closed, and interim stewardship
*Status: Decided (D-51; Art 31, 45, 64) · Old: Art 31, 45, 64 · First phase: S1*

Nothing enters public view until a moderation run has reviewed it under the ratified policy pack. Runs happen on submit, on every update (diff-aware, blocking for anything public), and after publication (on a policy change, a context change, and periodic sampling). Every run goes through the privacy gate (II.6) and the privacy gateway first.

- **Fail closed.** If moderation is unavailable, outdated, unsupported in the language, or below the decision point's confidence threshold, the item is held (`hold`): it stays in `draft` or `submitted` and never reaches `eligible`. The queue may be slow rather than lower the standard. Public exposure fails closed. Safety routing fails open (I.6).
- **Outcomes** are `publish`, `needs_revision`, `reject`, `route_external`, `hold` and `escalate_human`. Only emergency, crisis, and legal or law-enforcement cases go to a human, through a small logged lane. Humans change policy, not single outcomes (`NO-INSTANCE-OVERRIDE-1`).
- **Every moderation decision** carries `rule_ids[]`, a field or span reference, a `revision_hint` (null only if safety-sensitive), `policy_version`, `prompt_hash`, `model_id`, confidence, a run id, and `appealable_until`. The UI shows each hint next to its field and offers revise-and-resubmit, which keeps the draft.
- **Visible states** distinguish: withdrawn by user; revision requested; awaiting review; held; awaiting jurisdictional review; approved on appeal; rejected with reasons; closed for demonstrated abuse. Withdrawal and resubmission is allowed. Reposting to probe or evade safeguards is not, and revisions are compared using the salted fingerprint (II.6).
- **Re-moderation is never silent.** When a policy change or new context flips the outcome for a published item, the item shows "re-reviewed under policy vX" with the explanation and an appeal path (`REMOD-NOTICE-1`).
- **Appeals.** A user may appeal any rejection, revise or hold decision. An independent re-run uses a different model or prompt variant. A still-disputed appeal becomes a community label task (V.5, V.6). An unsuccessful good-faith appeal is never penalized.
- **Interim policy stewardship.** Until a ratifying panel and constitutional quorum exist, the founder ratifies policy pack v1 and each later version as transitional steward (VIII.2). Packs carry `approved_by` and `expires_at`, and decisions made under them show "Policy vX, transitional stewardship". Once a quorum ratifies a version, the transitional marking is removed from it. The same applies to appeal outcomes: the founder never decides a single appeal in place of the review path.

Rules: PUB-FAILCLOSED-1, FAIL-CLOSED-AI-1, MOD-EXPLAIN-1, NO-INSTANCE-OVERRIDE-1, REMOD-NOTICE-1, INTERIM-1, APPEAL-1, APPEAL-2, DRAFT-TTL-1, CRISIS-STATIC-1

### V.5 APPEALS-FEEDBACK: Appeals produce labeled examples that amend policy
*Status: Decided (D-51); Drafted (Art 44) · Old: Art 44 · First phase: S1*

A successful or disputed appeal does not edit a decision by hand, and it does not rewrite prompts, models or rules directly. It yields a labeled example or a rule change proposed as a pull request to the policy pack in `can_policy`. The amendment loop:

1. A proposal arrives as a PR. Automated evals run against the labeled sets, with a threshold per decision point.
2. A **replay diff** re-runs the proposed policy over a sample of past decisions and reports what would flip.
3. The community ratifies it. The method is an open question. Default: a randomized, context-masked, cross-jurisdiction review panel plus `can_policy` maintainers. Transitional founder stewardship approves v1 (VIII.2).
4. Staged rollout: shadow, then canary, then full, with rollback.
5. Affected content is re-moderated under the new version (V.4).
6. The appealed instance is re-decided by AI under the new version.

Before use, a candidate example needs privacy review and redaction, independent extra labeling, coordination and poisoning checks, failure analysis, regression tests, disparate-impact evaluation, and formal approval. An unsuccessful good-faith appeal carries no penalty, and penalties need separate evidence of deliberate abuse. Appeal outcomes write nothing to grounding tables directly. Design: `docs/design/ai/amendment-loop.md`, `docs/design/ai/appeals.md`.

**Simulation before public (D-55).** The first red team of a policy pack is AI personas (submitters, contributors, proposers, appellants, adversaries) driving full lifecycles through the real pipeline on seed problems with synthetic evidence. Their findings feed this loop like any other proposal. Public participation opens only after graduation criteria, agreed in advance, pass on the simulation (`docs/design/ai/simulation.md`). CI personas run on `FakeModel`; live persona runs are founder-gated.

Rules: APPEAL-1, SIM-GATE-1, SIM-LABEL-1, SIM-NOSECRET-1

### V.6 DISTRIBUTED-REVIEW: Distributed review: label tasks and ratification panels
*Status: Decided (Art 23); Drafted (Art 32) · Old: Art 23, 32 · First phase: S1, P5*

No single AI output, reviewer, popularity signal, or reputation score becomes final authority. Humans take part in two kinds of distributed review. **Label tasks** answer one labeling question about a masked example (appeals, eval sets, audit samples). **Ratification panels** approve policy changes. Both use: randomized and context-masked assignment; domain and jurisdiction matching where needed; conflict declaration and detection; independent labels before reviewers see the aggregate; multiple reviewers for consequential changes; disagreement measurement and escalation; fresh pools on appeal; calibration on known cases; provenance without exposing reviewer data. Reputation may shift confidence and is never proof that a person is right.

Consensus is more than a count. It weighs independence, competence, calibration, conflicts, evidence quality, contextual fit, and uncertainty. Disagreement is recorded, not forced into unanimity. For high-impact changes thresholds are set before labels are collected. Ties, low confidence, coordinated anomalies, and unresolved rights conflicts escalate. Bias is monitored across jurisdictions and the change history is public (anti-capture). Panel size and randomization are open questions. Until panels exist, transitional stewardship stands in (VIII.2).

Rules: NO-INSTANCE-OVERRIDE-1
